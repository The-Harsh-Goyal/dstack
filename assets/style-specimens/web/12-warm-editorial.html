Teahouse Nights

How many steeps before the pot starts telling the truth

The teahouse at the end of South Pond Lane has been open twenty-three years. Its keeper, Old Yu, says what guests talk about depends on how many steeps the tea has had.

Old Yu's ledger has no revenue line, only "which table stayed till when". He says the first two steeps are small talk, from the third they talk work, and by the fifth the table finally says what's really troubling them.

Behind the counter he keeps a small hourglass. It isn't for timing; it's for new staff: until it has turned three times, don't disturb any table.

So he never pushes a second pot. "Tea leaves are cheap," he says while warming the cups, "a seat where you can finish talking isn't."

"The first two steeps are small talk; only by the fifth does anyone tell the truth."— Old Yu
